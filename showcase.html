01 / THE IDEA

The octopus took a different path. Could a messenger?

simplex.chat has no user IDs and keeps contacts and groups on your phone. So why should it look like WhatsApp or Telegram? This project asks what an interface grown from SimpleX's own foundations could feel like.

Human relationships decide what the interface contains: people, groups, publications and providers. The octopus decides how it flows, in six rules.

  1. 01

    Coordinate

    One home keeps you oriented.

  2. 02

    Act locally

    Tools open where you are working.

  3. 03

    Adapt the space

    The screen makes room, then gives it back.

  4. 04

    Retain state

    Drafts, places and choices survive leaving.

  5. 05

    Cross deliberately

    Anything leaving a conversation is reviewed first.

  6. 06

    Return coherently

    You come back to where you were.

A design hypothesis about interaction, not a description of the SimpleX protocol or a biological simulation.

09 / V2.1.2.3NEW6 October

Photos you can talk about, email kept apart, a home that scales.

Today's edit answers four open issues from the project's own tracker, and fixes the website itself. Each one has a check that fails on the build before it.

  • Home at scale and motion. Today, This week and Earlier; twenty at a time; an Unread filter; names never shortened. A 0.2 second slide, none with reduced motion. 36 checks.
  • Photo pins. A reply pinned to a point on a photo, placed by touch, keyboard or by naming the detail, with a text list of every pin. 29 checks.
  • Photo tools. The camera sits beside the reply. Preview, queued, accepted and failed states, each with a way forward. 30 checks.
  • Email channels. The thread is shown, a different sender address is flagged as not verified, the attachment stays with the email, and forwarding is its own reviewed step. 31 checks.
  • The website. A homepage audit fixed eleven problems: one "latest", one navigation row, the app's own palette, and a live phone in the first screen. The type badge no longer covers a name's initials (36 checks), and the full-screen phone keeps phone width on a laptop.

10 / OPEN

What we do not know yet.

Everything above was built and checked in a browser. None of it has been used by a real person on a real phone. That is the next step, and the most important one.

  • Do people prefer the rail? The real-phone study compares the three homes with six to nine people. It is ready to run; no sessions have taken place.
  • Questions only SimpleX can answer. Five questions for the maintainers, on link previews, read status, forwarding across profiles and a directory source, are written up and unanswered.
  • Still to design. The profile edit screen, links between entities, group lifecycle, recovery after a reload, a tablet layout, and naming questions in the information architecture.

HOW IT IS CHECKED

Measured, then fixed, then held.

Every change follows one order: measure in the browser, fix the shared source, then add a check that fails on the old build. A fault seen twice becomes a rule. 30 check scripts now run against the prototype at phone and laptop widths, at text sizes up to 32 px, and with reduced motion.

Evidence level: browser simulation in headless Chromium. No physical device, screen reader or participant has been used. Sizes are CSS pixels. Large text means the browser's text setting, not iOS Dynamic Type or Android font scale. All people, messages and organisations are fictional; nothing is sent, and a reload clears everything. "Latest edit" does not mean production-ready or validated.
