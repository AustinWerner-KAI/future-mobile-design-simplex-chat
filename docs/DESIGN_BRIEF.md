# Design brief

## The purpose

Explore what the future SimpleX experience could become. This is a discovery project, not a current-app redesign brief or near-term feature backlog. Existing privacy, identity and connection concepts are foundations to respect and investigate, not a restriction to today’s interface.

## The job

Help someone move between a private conversation, a shared object and a small group activity without losing their place, draft or understanding of who can see what.

## The idea

A continuous surface gathers, reaches, opens and settles. The selected region becomes the workspace. Its shape should reveal function and state.

The octopus is a biological analogy, not a claim about collective animal behaviour or literal multiple independent brains. Local sensing/control inspires contextual tools; flexibility inspires adaptive surfaces; selective exposure inspires explicit audience boundaries. No mandatory eight-part menu or ornamental tentacles.

## Five principles

- Resolve complexity before simplifying appearance.
- Design the whole journey, including mistakes and interruptions.
- Make visual material respond in ways that match its appearance.
- Care for small details: labels, position, drafts, acceptance and delivery states.
- Give the person's content the greatest presence. Design has no ego.

These are our working interpretations of design principles discussed through Jony Ive's work, not quotations or an endorsement.

## Required boundaries

Chat and email can share organisation while retaining their actual channel properties. A recipient must accept an invitation before membership changes. Joining a plan and confirming its date are separate. Sharing a plan does not share private messages or photo replies. Sent content may be retained by recipients. A visible contour does not enforce access control.

SimpleX's protocol and membership model require engineering review before any production integration. The current demo is not evidence that a proposed security model is feasible.

## Explore, then validate

Test draft preservation, source retrieval, audience comprehension, invitation acceptance and channel selection. Include many chats, one-handed use, large text, reduced motion, offline delivery and screen readers. Record success, mistakes and uncertainty. No user-study results exist yet.

## First milestone

One coherent, accessible 60-second journey: open Maya, write, share a photo, attach a reply, propose a plan, invite someone, return home and find the source again. Email is a clearly labelled adjoining scenario.
