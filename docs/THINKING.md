# The Future of Mobile Design for SimpleX Chat

## Why I started

I started with a simple question: what could the mobile chat menu become by 2028?

I wanted it organised, clean to the eye, generous with space and genuinely forward-looking. My phrase for the project became **“design has no ego.”** The interface should give people and their conversations the greatest presence.

The first attempts looked like familiar messaging products with a new visual treatment. I kept asking: what actually makes this innovative? What considerations are we making? A future date on a polished mockup was not enough.

This repository takes you through that exploration and invites you to help improve it.

## My design lens

Jony Ive's work prompted a set of working principles: solve complexity before simplifying appearance; design the whole experience; understand the material; care for unnoticed details; let usefulness guide form.

I am using those principles as questions, not borrowing his authority to approve the concept. We cannot know what he would think of it. The strongest question for our work is: does each curve, movement and control earn its place?

## The five predictions

These are hypotheses for exploration, not promised features or forecasts with measured confidence.

1. **A living present.** A conversation could offer a view of current proposals, open questions and shared objects, with source messages available behind it.
2. **Tools at the point of action.** Useful controls could unfold beside the thing someone touches, then recede.
3. **Images as shared places.** People could discuss a particular detail directly on a photograph instead of referring to it in an unrelated message.
4. **Privacy people can read.** The interface could make invitation scope, audiences and membership visible before sharing.
5. **A quiet return.** A person could resume from a meaningful source message without feeling obliged to process every unread item first.

Casual affection, jokes and conversation remain important. A living present must not turn every relationship into a task board.

## Why the octopus

I wanted to draw experiences from good design in nature and move outside the standard model. The octopus became the most interesting inspiration: flexible anatomy, precise contact, extensive arm circuitry and remarkable control of its visible surface.

The biological sources explain the inspiration; the interface mappings below are our design interpretations.

| Observation | Design interpretation | What to try |
|---|---|---|
| Flexible arms can bend along their length | An interface can adapt locally around activity | Expand the selected region without discarding its origin |
| Extensive arm circuitry supports sensing and movement | Tools can respond where the interaction happens | Reveal Photo or Write beside the relevant object |
| Suckers provide many contact points | A reply can attach to a specific detail | Put a conversation point on an image |
| Colour and texture can change rapidly | The visible surface can change with state | Quiet navigation, active workspace, explicit audience |
| Several arms work within a single body | Different activities can share a coherent environment | Preserve drafts and context across tools |

The octopus is not a “most evolved” animal; evolution is not a ladder. “Many brains” is shorthand that should not become our technical premise. Its social behaviour is not the basis for our group features. We are borrowing functional questions from anatomy, not claiming the animal is a model community.

Sources: [University of Chicago: octopus nervous-system anatomy](https://biologicalsciences.uchicago.edu/news/unique-octopus-nervous-system) and [Smithsonian Ocean: changes in colour and texture](https://ocean.si.edu/ocean-life/invertebrates/how-octopuses-and-squids-change-color).

## The design language

**Gather → reach → open → settle.** That sequence is the heart of the concept.

A named relationship remains recognisable. Touching it opens the same region into a conversation. An image expands into a shared surface. Tools emerge locally. Leaving restores the prior context and keeps the draft.

The visual vocabulary is restrained: warm white, deep petrol ink, shallow relief, readable type, generous space and a small number of meaningful contours. Boundaries should describe audiences. Movement should explain transitions. Neither should become decoration for its own sake.

The phone's rectangular display is a constraint we can work with. Our current work is about fluid interface behaviour, not a claim that flexible or organic hardware will be ready by 2028.

## The concepts and what changed

Early directions offered cleaner lists, spaces and lenses. They were easy to recognise, but did not yet express a different interaction model.

The sculptural surface study introduced Rest, Connect, Unfold and Share. It made the octopus influence more visible through continuous folds and branching paths.

The five-behaviour board then mapped the predictions to one visual family. The storyboard followed invitations, writing, choosing and sending a photograph, discussing a detail, gathering a plan and returning.

Finally, the working prototype tested opening and closing a conversation, draft preservation, local image replies, visible sharing scope and explicit acceptance. Email was added as a future integration scenario: the relationship can organise both channels while preserving the distinction between them.

Concept boards are AI-generated visual studies. They contain inconsistencies and should not be mistaken for a finished product specification. The editable prototype is also exploratory.

## The critique I want everyone to see

The design became more convincing when we admitted that organic outlines alone were not enough.

The home still resembles rounded rows. Several flows expand from a generic region instead of their real source. Large contact sets, screen readers and real protocol permissions remain unresolved. Drafts survive local navigation; full reload restoration across every flow does not yet work consistently.

The task now is to earn the form through behaviour. We need to test whether people can discover actions, reverse them, understand the audience and retain their place. Beautiful motion cannot excuse uncertainty about sending or access.

## Why SimpleX

SimpleX's public description emphasises a network without user IDs and contacts and groups held on the user's device. Those constraints make a useful foundation for exploring a coherent private communication experience. [SimpleX](https://simplex.chat/)

This is an independent project, not an official SimpleX redesign or roadmap. Real integration needs the maintainers' technical input. Email integration is a scenario from this project's originating conversation, not a verified commitment by the founder. Ordinary email retains its actual privacy and delivery properties.

## An invitation

I want passionate designers, researchers and builders to add their thinking, challenge the assumptions and help make the experience work.

Bring a sketch, a motion study, a useful critique, an accessibility finding or a small working interaction. Start with one problem. Show what improves for the person using it, and how we can evaluate the difference.

The direction is shared; the answer is open. I would rather learn from a thoughtful alternative than protect a visual simply because it was the first one I liked.
