## The human problem to investigate

A person arranging something with a friend or business may have to reconstruct the same context in a separate tool. Our proposal is that the relationship remains the home of the task. We have not measured the frequency or cost of this problem. T05 and a later comparison must establish whether the proposed workspace is useful.

Human relationships organise the interface. The octopus informs the flow: a useful extension stays attached to its source, acts locally, and folds back without losing context. Organic decoration alone would not establish this behaviour.

## Alternatives considered

| Approach | What changes for the person | Largest unknown |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Conversation workspace — recommended | A booking or comparison unfolds inside one open chat | Can people distinguish local preparation from sending? |
| Relationship connections | A person can deliberately link a café, place and group in their own organisation | Do links imply a public affiliation or expose other connections? |
| Portable result | A chosen detail can become a reviewed message to another relationship | Does its content or attribution reveal its source? |
| Return marker | A paused task leaves a recognisable way back to the draft and object | Does it reduce searching or add clutter? |
| Remove the tool | Ordinary messages and client-rendered choices complete the task with no expandable workspace | Is the workspace unnecessary overhead? |
| Person-selected workspace | The person chooses the useful tool rather than accepting a sender's interface | Does this improve control at the cost of extra setup? |

The recommended experiment compares the workspace with the simpler ordinary-message condition. We do not claim these ideas are unprecedented. The project contribution is their proposed combination under relationship continuity and deliberate disclosure.

## First scene: Harbour Café

1. **Receive:** a fictional café reply offers a table-choice interface. Sender and purpose are visible. The existing conversation and draft remain available.
2. **Unfold:** the person opens the offer beside its source. It shows fictional times and party sizes. No selections are sent.
3. **Choose locally:** choosing a time changes only this object. Available space grows on request; the interface does not rearrange the person's home.
4. **Review:** a client-owned review shows the exact outgoing request, Harbour Café as recipient and the active identity. The sender's content cannot draw or authorise this step in the proposed architecture.
5. **Commit:** only the person submits. A browser study labels the result as a simulated request, not a confirmed booking, payment or delivered message.
6. **Fold back:** the workspace returns to its original message. A concise result remains; the prior draft, reading position and focus return.
7. **Cancel or fail:** cancellation shares nothing; failure preserves the selection for review/retry. Repeating the action cannot silently create a second request.

No real business, model, booking, transport, payment, downloaded code or cryptographic security is required for this initial simulation. Any future production enforcement requires a separate engineering and security design.

## Evidence and release gates

T05 remains the next live research activity: 6–9 consenting people on their own phones. T22 remains gated by T05 and T21. This brief prepares the next experiment without introducing another untested visual version.

Before Study A, define the outcome counts: who understands that a local choice has not been sent; who identifies recipient and identity; who cancels without sending; who recovers the original draft. Record hints and wrong turns. Preference alone does not establish an improvement.

Compare equivalent tasks and wording with ordinary messages. Test cancellation, exact outgoing payload, retry duplication, focus, large text, reduced motion and screen-reader announcements. Browser checks demonstrate defined behaviour, not participant comprehension, native integration or isolation of untrusted content.

Drop or simplify the workspace if it adds task effort, obscures the audience, suggests a confirmed booking, or makes return harder. Keep the ordinary-message alternative viable.

## Octopus check

- **Flow rules:** Act locally; Return coherently.
- **SimpleX foundation:** one open relationship and deliberate sending are the starting point; programmable interfaces are a proposed addition, not a verified capability.
- **Analogy:** an extension operates beside its source and returns without losing that context; no literal anatomy or social-behaviour claim.
- **Test planned:** local choices emit no outgoing payload; cancel emits none; reviewed commit emits one; closing restores the draft and focus. The UI-free contract now tests these intended transitions; there is no integrated frame, enforced security boundary or participant result yet.
- **Research:** [Interfaces that arrive §9.1 F5/F7/F8/F9](RESEARCH_NOTES/INTERFACES_THAT_ARRIVE.md#91-principles), [§10.2 sequence](RESEARCH_NOTES/INTERFACES_THAT_ARRIVE.md#102-sequence-of-studies), [§10.4 gates](RESEARCH_NOTES/INTERFACES_THAT_ARRIVE.md#104-decisions-this-paper-does-not-make).
