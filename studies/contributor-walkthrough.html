Before you start

We’re testing the project, not you. Use your usual device and browser. Say what you’re looking for and what you expect to happen. You can skip a task or stop at any time.

Start at the repository. Try one task at a time. Don’t read the facilitator checks until you finish. Use fictional content in the prototype and stop before submitting an issue or sending a message.

Optional notes below stay only in this open page. Nothing is uploaded or saved by this project. Copy notes you want to keep before leaving. Avoid personal details.

01 / UNDERSTAND

What is this project?

You’ve just found this project. Explain what it is trying to explore and who it belongs to.

02 / TRACE

How did the thinking change?

Find where the design thinking began and one later change. What prompted that change?

03 / LOCATE

Which experience is current?

Find the experience the project is currently asking people to explore. How did you decide it was current?

04 / TRY

Can you return to your words?

Write an unsent reply to Maya, explore a tool in that relationship, then return to your words. Tell us what happened. Do not send.

05 / BUILD

Where would you work?

You want to work on the connection invitation. Find its latest visual and the file you would edit.

06 / CONTRIBUTE

What would you investigate?

Find an unresolved question you could investigate. Show where you would propose your contribution. Stop before submitting anything.

Before you finish

What was hardest to work out? What information would you need before making your first contribution?

If you choose to share a finding, describe the version, action and consequence. Public GitHub submissions should omit personal details.

For the facilitator

Let the participant find the route. Don’t explain the thesis or point to the answer first.

Ask neutral prompts: “What are you looking for?” or “What makes you say that?” Record any help you give.

What to observe

Version choices, backtracking, uncertainty, draft loss and whether the person can find the contribution route.

Separate actions and quotes from your interpretation. Disagreement with the concept is not a failed task.

Session arrangements

Agree to notes before starting. Recording needs a separate choice; notes are enough if it’s declined.

No participants are currently recruited. Scheduling and any compensation remain unset.

Facilitator checks — open after the tasks

Understand, then compare

  1. Independent speculative project, rather than official SimpleX work or a production app.
  2. Anatomy is the opening explanation; the actual creation order and reasons for change remain visible. The evolutionary analogy inspires behaviour.
  3. The current inline experience is distinguished from V2 foundations and historical studies.
  4. The unsent draft remains in page memory across local tools; no real sending or reload persistence is assumed.
  5. V2 Connect’s preview and shared editable source are located; generated output is distinguished from source.
  6. A scoped proposal/finding route and an evidence plan are found. Coding is optional.

A different navigation route can succeed. These are interpretation checks, not directions for where someone must click.

After each session record the issue, consequence, supporting task or quote and possible fix. Compare patterns and exceptions after the round. A small convenience sample cannot establish population percentages.

Publish only an appropriately anonymised summary; attributed quotes need permission. Results remain “not collected” until sessions happen.
