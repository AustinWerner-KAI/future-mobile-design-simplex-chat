Why grids?

A place for the relationships you choose. A hypothesis to test, not a rule for every inbox.

Human relationships organise the interface.

The octopus theory is the flow architecture: coordination, local action, adaptable space, retained context, deliberate crossing and coherent return. Grids, strips and collections are alternative ways into that same flow.

Stable anchors, useful activity

A small favourites grid gives familiar names and identity marks a place before asking which transport to use. Our hypothesis is that this can help repeated recognition. A recent-activity list still carries message previews and a larger inbox. We have not established that a grid is faster or better through participant research.

The trade-offs matter

  • Grid: three initial favourites, expandable to six, plus a list option. More visible anchors cost vertical space. Order follows the fictional directory.
  • Strip: leaves room for activity but hides some favourites initially. Arrow buttons and search offer alternatives.
  • Collections: you can create, rename and overlap personal contexts. Organisation takes effort and never changes who receives a message.

Long names and enlarged text can change positions. Spatial recognition cannot replace names, search, clear type labels or accessible controls. Monograms contain no QR data or globally unique identifier.

Test the flow, not just the picture

  1. Find Maya and write an unsent draft.
  2. Explore a proposal, then return. Check the draft and reading position.
  3. Open Coast Journal and explain the audience and whether replies are possible.
  4. Prepare an email excerpt from Harbour Café; review the exact text and destination before simulated sharing.
  5. Find someone beyond the visible favourites. In collections, place Maya in two contexts without changing any audience.

Compare finding time, wrong selections, lost context and audience misunderstandings with participants, varying the order of candidates. Include many conversations, long names, enlarged text and assistive input.

What works here

The gallery contains playable previews and full-screen links. Each runs independently. Drafts and changes live only in that page session. Reload clears them. Sending, invitations and email are fictional simulations; activity counts remain fixtures. Physical-device keyboards, screen readers and participant outcomes remain unverified.
