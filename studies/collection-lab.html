Study · grids, lists and carousels · lab 1, rebuilt · 7 October 2026

People stay where you put them.

The first lab built a carousel well but never asked when a carousel is right. Public SimpleX issues and published research answered: people want a fixed place, their own order, and a way to move through items that works with a screen reader. This lab is rebuilt on those findings.

Independent design study, not affiliated with SimpleX. Browser simulation; fictional people and messages, real licensed photos; nothing is sent. Evidence: public SimpleX issues and published studies, listed with each check below. No participant has used this yet.

Flow rules served
Adapt the working space (the collection takes the form the space, the text size and the person need) and Retain useful state (your order, your place and your focus survive every change).
From SimpleX
Lists exist since 6.3 and favourites are previews already in memory, so grid and list are cheap. An album is one chat. The frame stays client-owned, so the client, not the sender, decides how options are shown (F14).
From the analogy
An arm reshapes to fit the space it reaches into but stays the same arm: the collection changes shape, never its order. Nothing literal.
The test
Does the collection change form when space or text size changes, without losing place or selection? scripts/check-collection-lab.cjs runs H0 to H14 here and fails on the first carousel lab.

Limits

Touch is synthesised in a browser, not tested on a phone in a hand. Screen reader structure is checked from the markup, not with VoiceOver or TalkBack. The evidence is a handful of public issues and studies of other software; it is a signal, not a measure. T05 with SimpleX users comes next.
