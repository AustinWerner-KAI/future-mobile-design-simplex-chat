A carousel that holds its place.

People still want grids, carousels and lists. They are not new, so what matters is how well they work. This lab builds one carousel and uses it twice: for the people you keep close, and inside an interface a business sends into a conversation. Every hard case has a check.

Independent design study, not affiliated with SimpleX. Browser simulation; fictional people and messages; nothing is sent. Research: Interfaces That Arrive §1, §8.2, §9.1 and §10.1.

Flow rules served
Coordinate (find someone and come back to the same place) and Act locally (choose from options inside the reply that offered them).
From SimpleX
Favourites already exist, and a strip of them only rearranges chat previews that are already in memory. A component the app owns can render any interface a contact sends, with no reviewer needed (F14).
From the analogy
Arms act on their own but stay attached to one centre: each card acts locally, and the strip always returns to a stable order. Nothing literal.
The test
Can someone find an entity and return to where they were? Can someone act without rebuilding the context? scripts/check-carousel-lab.cjs runs H1 to H10 here and fails on today's rail.
What would drop it

Limits

Touch is synthesised in a browser, not tested on a phone in a hand. Screen reader behaviour is checked from the markup, not with VoiceOver or TalkBack. Nobody has used this yet: T05 comes first. Grids and lists follow in labs 2 and 3.
